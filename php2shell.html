Is your PHP version fixed?

Type the version from php -v. The check runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.

  • 8.5 8.5.12
  • 8.4 8.4.27
  • 8.3 8.3.36
  • 8.2 8.2.35
Waiting for a versionTry 8.4.26 or 8.4.27.
  • Linux distributions often fix PHP without changing the version number. Ubuntu 24.04 still reports 8.3.6 after its fix, so check your distribution's advisory.
  • The fix only counts after PHP is fully restarted. Many servers run several copies of PHP, and php -v shows only the command-line one.
  • A fixed version does not cover CVE-2026-115560. Keep session.upload_progress.enabled off.

What to do about it

Three moves, in this order.

  1. Update PHP

    Move to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35, or install your distribution's patched package. PHP 8.1 and older get no official release: use a vendor backport or upgrade.

  2. Restart it fully

    Restart PHP-FPM and the web server, then check every copy: hosting panels, containers and appliances often ship their own PHP.

  3. Close the second door

    Attackers chained PHP2Shell with a Linux kernel bug to get root. Patch the kernel, and take management interfaces off the public internet.

Also look back: the first attempt hit Amazon's honeypots on October 17, four days before the patch. Check logs and web folders from that date.

How it unfolded

  1. October 17

    Amazon's honeypots see the first attempt, four days before the fix.

  2. October 20

    Details leak early. PHP posts an advance notice of an emergency release.

  3. October 21

    Fixes ship for 8.5, 8.4, 8.3 and 8.2. F5 Labs records thousands of attempts within hours.

  4. October 23

    PHP flags a third issue (CVE-2026-115560) that the update does not fix. Keep session upload progress off.

  5. October 23 to 24

    Dream reports 24 Palo Alto firewalls rooted by chaining PHP2Shell with a Linux kernel bug, Fragnesia (CVE-2026-46300). Unit 42 verified the tooling.

  6. October 26

    CISA adds both CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

  7. November 3

    Singapore's CSA warns about attacks on management interfaces, naming Palo Alto, Cisco, Citrix, F5 and Siemens.

  8. November 12

    Check Point reports a chain against its gateways, in attacks between October 30 and November 4.

Reality check. Most traffic is still scanning and failed attempts. Wiz found a working attack must be tuned to each PHP build, and Fastly found other campaigns counted under PHP2Shell signatures. The confirmed break-ins are real, though. Scale: about 2.6 million internet-facing PHP services, roughly 73.5% on PHP 8.1 or older (Mallory). Exposed vulnerable machines fell about 67% in the first week (Wiz). Checked November 13, 2026.
